Option A

Flipchart

All sections share one motion: top hinge, bottom edge lifts.

Every transition uses the same motion — every section is hinged at its top edge and lifts the same way Hero does today. Six identical page-lifts as you scroll through the card.

Pro Simplest to build; the existing HeroFold code can be parameterized and re-used five more times.
Con Repetitive after the first; the metaphor doesn't really say "physical card" — it says "stack of pages bound at the top, like a notepad."

Option B

Accordion (Z-fold)

Hinges alternate per section — top, then bottom, then top.

III A MEMORY album
II ↑ from bottom THE THREE milestones
I · cover ↑ from top MÉLANGE — invites you to —

Hero lifts at the top hinge (current). Milestones is connected to Hero at its TOP edge and to Gallery at its BOTTOM edge — so to reveal Gallery, Milestones swings up and back at its bottom hinge (top edge moves away from the viewer). Then Gallery → Details flips at the top again. The card "unfolds" like a real Z-fold pamphlet.

Pro Strongest "this is a physical card" feel; matches your "opposite side / same side" mental model exactly.
Con Two timeline variants to maintain instead of one; bottom-hinge motion can feel like the page is "falling forward" if not tuned carefully.

Option C

Slide with crease

Panel translates upward; a faint pink crease hints at the fold.

No 3D rotation. The leaving panel translates straight upward, and a faint pink hairline + soft drop-shadow appear briefly at its bottom edge as it slides — implying the seam where the paper was folded, without actually animating a fold. Lighter weight; the "card" is suggested, not demonstrated.

Pro Cheapest to produce, smoothest on low-end devices, easy to chain five times without feeling repetitive.
Con Doesn't really say "I am turning a page" — more "I am scrolling a long document with paper-styled section breaks."
