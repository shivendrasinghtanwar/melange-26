candidates.

The hero is the closed cover of the invitation; on scroll, the card opens and reveals The Three Milestones inside. Book Cover and Envelope are out. Bi-fold has been re-tuned so the doors stay visible on mobile too. Top-fold replaced with bottom-fold (the card drops open downward instead of lifting up).

I

Bi-fold doors.

A thin vertical seam runs down the centre of the hero. As you scroll, the two halves rotate outward — left half swings left, right half swings right — exposing The Three Milestones in the middle.

How it feels: the classic Indian invitation-card opening. Symmetric, ceremonial.
Mobile: the doors stay visible — perspective is gently reduced and the rotation softens to 100° so the two narrower halves still clear the inside cleanly.

II

Bottom-fold open.

The hinge is at the top edge of the hero. As you scroll, the card rotates around that top edge so the bottom swings downward and forward — the card "drops open" — revealing The Three Milestones underneath.

How it feels: matches the direction of scroll (you scroll down, the card opens down). The simplest, most universally legible of the openings, and identical behaviour on mobile and desktop.
Mobile: identical — no fallback needed, single-edge rotation works perfectly at 375px.

How to evaluate: open one full-screen, scroll slowly, watch the card open as you go. Try at desktop and at mobile width (Chrome DevTools → device toolbar → iPhone). Reduced-motion preference is honoured — the animation is skipped if the OS asks for it.

Scroll mechanics in both: the hero stays pinned (position: sticky) for one additional viewport-height of scroll. During that scroll the opening transform progresses from 0 → 1. After it finishes, normal flow resumes into The Three Milestones (then Gallery, Particulars, RSVP, Footer — exactly as the site already scrolls today).
